Why it exists

Agents can do the work. Deciding stays with you.

Most of what surrounds a change, reading it, answering feedback, rebasing a stack, turning a ticket into a PR, an agent can now do. What it shouldn't do is decide for you. Concierge gives that work to agents and brings back only what needs you: a finding to post, an answer to give, a push to approve.

It brings the tools you already use into one place, and replaces none of them

Not a review tool

Line-by-line review stays in crit. Concierge starts a crit session for each review, lets the agent write its findings there, and shows you what is waiting.

Not an agent

Claude Code, Codex, Copilot or a local Llama does the work, on your own plans and keys. Concierge decides what to run, where, and when to stop and ask.

Not a tracker

GitHub and Linear stay the source of truth. Concierge reads them, and writes there only what you approve.

Modes

One workspace, for whoever you are today.

Work as a developer, a product manager, or both. Each mode has its own workspaces, skills and setup, and switching takes one click.

What it does

What it does.

Reviews

When someone requests your review, an agent prepares it. You read the findings, then post or approve with one click.

Your PRs

On your own PRs, one click works through the review comments. Another pushes and replies to each thread.

Explain

What changes for users, and which systems it touches, drawn on a living map of the whole codebase.

Ship

Your Linear tickets and the GitHub issues assigned to you, on one board. Pick a few, and agents ship them as stacked or parallel PRs.

Standup

What shipped, what's in review and what waits on you, gathered from the day's work.

Plans

Write a brief, and an agent breaks it down with you into epics and user stories, drawn live as one roadmap to the goal. Play with it: try moves and plays, see the date move, then apply to Linear.

Before you start

What to be careful with.

Everything stays on your machine, in plain files your user account can read. The agents run as you, so they can read them too.

Keys are plain text

In ~/.config/concierge/secrets.json and your own mcp.env, scrubbed from logs but readable by the agents. Use the narrowest keys that work, keep disk encryption on, and rotate a key the moment it shows up where it shouldn't.

Runs go to their provider

Code, tickets, PR discussions and whatever a connection returns are sent to the account that runs the agent. Use accounts your organisation has approved, and be careful with production data.

Agents act as you

They push, comment and update tickets with your access, and their sessions keep what was said in ~/.local/state/concierge. Nothing goes public without your click.

Install

Getting started.

Clone and run one command

bin/up asks for the folder with your clones, builds and starts it. No tokens in files.

It opens by itself

At 127.0.0.1:4747. On Docker Desktop it checks host networking and walks you through turning it on.

Pick repos, sign in

A short setup signs you in to GitHub and an agent, finds your repos, and clones the ones you don't have yet.

Terminal
$ gh repo clone ylkhayat/concierge && cd concierge
$ bin/up                 # sign-ins happen in the app

Need skills to start with? ylkhayat/skills has review-pr, revise, ship-tickets and the rest Concierge runs: paste its link in the setup's Skills step. Bring your own repositories of skills the same way, as many as you like.

No Docker? Run ./install.sh instead.

Contributing

Contributing.

To make it work differently, fork it: swap in your own skills, change the prompts, or add a workspace. Its whole look lives in one file, src/design/tokens.css. Issues and pull requests are welcome.
